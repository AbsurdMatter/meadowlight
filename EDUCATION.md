# EDUCATION.md — Facts & Tone

All in-game facts come from this file (or are verified before adding).
Phrase every fact warmly, concretely, in one or two sentences, with a number
where possible. Never lecture; imagine a beekeeper friend chatting over a fence.

## Field notebook facts (ready to use)

- Honeybee workers visit 50–100 flowers per foraging trip, and a colony can
  visit millions of flowers in a year.
- More than 70% of bee species are solitary — they don't live in hives, and
  most are gentle stingers because they have no colony to defend.
- A single mason bee can pollinate as many flowers as roughly 100 honeybees —
  wild bees often outperform hives at pollination.
- A shallow dish of water with pebbles gives bees a safe place to drink;
  without a perch, they can drown in even a birdbath.
- Lavender at peak bloom can feed dozens of bumblebees at once — plant enough
  and you'll hear the patch before you see it.
- A meadow of one flower species is a feast-then-famine buffet; bees need
  blooms from early spring to late autumn.
- Leaving a corner of the meadow unmown gives wild bees nesting sites and
  butterflies a place to overwinter.
- In winter, the honeybee colony clusters around the queen and shivers to
  keep her at a cozy ~21°C, feeding on stored honey.
- Bees can see ultraviolet — many flowers have landing-strip patterns
  invisible to us, guiding bees straight to the nectar.
- One in three bites of food depends on pollinators.
- Bumblebees can shake pollen loose by buzzing at the right frequency —
  tomatoes rely on this "buzz pollination."
- A queen bumblebee starts the colony alone in spring, incubating her first
  eggs like a tiny bird.

## Tone rules

- Warm, curious, never preachy or alarmist.
- Positive framing: "here's what helps" not "the world is dying."
- Concrete numbers beat vague superlatives.
- One fact per interaction; depth on click-through, never forced.
